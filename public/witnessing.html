What is witnessing?

A Live Verify check asks the issuer, right now, whether it stands behind a document. Witnessing answers a later question: can you still prove the issuer said yes, after it has stopped saying so?

Status: a design, not a service. Witnessing sits outside the core Live Verify specification. No witnessing organisation operates today, the reference apps have no witness plugin, and any company names or prices on these pages are illustrations.

The gap it closes

A verified document is only as durable as the issuer's willingness to keep answering. An issuer that comes to regret a claim — a bookmaker facing a large payout, an employer who wishes a reference had not been written, a contractor disowning certified work — can remove the hash from its server. The lookup then finds nothing, and the issuer says it never issued the document.

The holder still has the text, and can show what hash it produces. What they cannot show is that the issuer ever published that hash. It is one party's word against the other's. A witness is a third party who looked at the time and wrote down what it saw.

The witness nobody had to call

The pattern is older than Live Verify. In London's over-the-counter gold market around 2001, dealers at member banks agreed trades by phone. The confirmation — metal, weight, fineness, price, delivery date — went to both counterparties and was copied to a firm in Switzerland whose only job was to keep it. The Swiss firm took no part in the trade and did not need to understand it. If the two sides ever disagreed about price or quantity, its copy would settle the matter.

A bullion trader who worked that desk described the arrangement to Live Verify's author, with a quiet punchline: nobody had ever asked the Swiss firm to adjudicate. Both sides knew a neutral copy existed and that they could not win a dispute against it, so the disputes did not happen.

This is the point of a witness. It stands ready to give evidence as an expert witness, and its value lies in being credible enough that it is never called. An issuer that knows a dated, independent record exists has no reason to delete a hash and deny it, because the denial would fail. A witness doing its job well produces very few attestations.

The weakness of the old arrangement was that either side could claim the copied message had been altered. A hash removes that argument: the witness never holds the text, and any change to the text changes the hash.

How it works

A witness is a company the person checking has chosen in advance and added to their Live Verify app as a plugin. Each time that person verifies a document:

  1. The app sends the witness the hash and the address it was looked up at. It does not send the document's text.
  2. The witness makes its own lookup at the issuer's domain, so its record is something it saw for itself and not something it was told.
  3. The witness stores what came back: the hash, the address, the issuer's answer, the chain of authorities above the issuer, and the time.
  4. Usually nothing more happens. If a dispute arises years later, the witness provides a formal statement of what it recorded.

Witnessing is a dated web lookup made by a credible third party. The technical bar is low. The value is the organisation's credibility, its record-keeping, and its willingness to stand behind the record later.

What the witness holds, and what it never holds

  • Holds: the hash, the address it was seen at, the issuer's response, the authority chain, and the time of the lookup.
  • Never holds: the document's text. A hash cannot be turned back into the document, so the witness cannot read what it has witnessed.
  • Does learn: that someone checked a document from that issuer at that time. A witness is a party you choose to tell, and should be chosen with that in mind.

What a witness can and cannot say later

It can say: “On this date, at this time, we looked up this hash at this issuer's address and received this answer.” Together with the holder's copy of the text, which produces that same hash, this establishes that the issuer confirmed the document.

It cannot say:

  • What the document said. It never had the text.
  • Who the rightful holder is. It does not know whose document it is.
  • That the issuer's claim was true. It records that the issuer answered, not that the issuer was right.
  • What the issuer said afterwards. A record shows the answer at the moment of the lookup; an issuer may properly revoke later.

Timing, and more than one witness

The record has to be made before the issuer has a reason to withdraw the hash. Once a dispute has started it is too late. That is why the plugin model matters: the record is made as a by-product of the ordinary check, without anyone having to foresee the dispute.

Nothing stops a person using two or three witnesses. Independent records are harder to dismiss than one, and each is only another lookup.

Two ways a witness comes to hold a record

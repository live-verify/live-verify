WITNESSING

How witnesses are paid

The Live Verify standard is free and stays free. Witnessing is a service that independent firms may choose to offer on top of it, competing on price, reputation and jurisdiction.

Status: a design, not a service. Witnessing sits outside the core Live Verify specification. No witnessing organisation operates today, the reference apps have no witness plugin, and any company names or prices on these pages are illustrations.

Two things are being bought

  • Recording and retention. Making the lookup and keeping the record for as long as a dispute could arise, often seven to ten years. Each record is tiny, so the cost per record is a small fraction of a cent a year.
  • Attestation. A formal, signed statement of what was recorded, for a court, a regulator or the parties to a dispute, and occasionally testimony. This is rare, takes a person's time, and carries the firm's reputation.

Most records are never called on, and that is the service working, not failing. As with the Swiss firm in the London gold market that was never once asked to adjudicate, a credible witness deters the dispute it exists to settle. The economics follow from that: recording is high-volume and nearly free; attestation and expert testimony are rare and priced like other custodian-of-records statements.

Who pays

  • Firms with a duty to show they checked. Lenders, letting agents, employers and expense auditors need to demonstrate later that a document verified on the day they relied on it. For them a witness record is a compliance record, bought on a business account.
  • Individuals who expect a dispute. Someone holding a document from an issuer they do not fully trust can pay for a record, or have their solicitor or insurer do it.
  • Issuers, where a regulator requires it. See issuer-fed witnessing.

Ways of charging

  • Prepaid bundles or business accounts. Amounts as small as a per-record fee cannot be billed one at a time, so recording is sold in bulk or by subscription.
  • A fee per attestation. Charged when a formal statement is requested.
  • Expert-witness fees. Recording is cheap or free and the firm earns when its testimony is needed, which gives it a direct interest in impeccable records.
  • Bundled with legal or accountancy services. A firm already retained adds witnessing at trivial marginal cost.
  • Alongside insurance. A legal-expenses or indemnity insurer witnesses the documents it may later have to defend.
  • Inside a subscription people already have. A platform company such as Apple, Google or Microsoft could include witnessing in a monthly plan, free or paid: as a new allowance, or rolled into an existing storage or productivity subscription. The billing relationship is already there, which removes the problem of charging tiny amounts. None of them offers this or has been asked to.

Who is credible as a witness

Witnessing is a credibility problem more than a technology problem. Candidates include solicitors and notaries, accountancy firms, archival services, and specialist firms set up for the purpose. What they compete on:

  • Standing and accreditation in the jurisdiction where a dispute would be heard.
  • Continuity. The record has to outlast the dispute window, so arrangements for what happens if the firm closes are part of the product.
  • How checkable its records are. See how a witness proves its own honesty.
  • What it learns. A witness sees who checked which issuer and when, so its own privacy practice matters.

Platform companies are candidates too. They already hold records for billions of accounts and are likely to outlast most disputes. Three conditions apply to them in particular:

  • Opt-in. A built-in witness is switched on by the user and is one choice among several. It is never a default nobody can change.
  • Independently certified software. The platform makes the software that sees the document, so its word that the text never leaves the device is not enough. An independent security firm would examine the build and state that it neither stores nor transmits the text of what is checked. That statement can itself be a Live Verify claim: issued on the security firm's own domain, naming the SHA-256 of the build it examined, and withdrawn if a later finding contradicts it. It is a claim by a named firm that can be held to it, not a proof, and it covers that build only.
  • What it still learns. Even with no text, a witness sees which issuers a person checks and when. For a company that already knows a great deal about its users, the policy on that record matters more than for a small specialist firm.

Why it is a market and not a fee

Any firm can offer witnessing, any person can choose which witness to use or to use none, and a check works identically either way. Nothing in the standard depends on a witness, and no witness has a privileged position. That keeps the public-good part free while leaving room for businesses to earn from the part that needs people, premises and a reputation.

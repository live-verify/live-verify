## The Problem

An intermediary says: "I submitted your application", "the supplier accepted your order", "your hours reached payroll", or "I introduced that customer". The person relying on this statement often has only a forwarded email, a screenshot or a document on the intermediary's letterhead.

The missing evidence is usually **what the intermediary did, for whom, with whose authority, and what the next party acknowledged**. The commission amount need not appear in that evidence. An honest intermediary can benefit as much as the customer: receipts demonstrate work, defend against blame for another party's delay, and support an agreed commission claim.

This is a global pattern across domestic and cross-border commerce, business services and employment. The cases below are proposed applications of Live Verify, not claims that the named sectors already issue these proofs. Local rules determine licensing, fee disclosure, worker protections and the legal effect of each document.

## Four Different Claims

| Claim | Appropriate issuer | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Authority:** "Agent A may act for Principal P on matter M" | Principal granting authority | The principal's stated appointment, scope and dates | That A took any action |
| **Action:** "We sent submission S to Recipient R" | Intermediary | An attributable statement of what it says it did | Independent confirmation of delivery |
| **Receipt:** "We received S from A for P" | Receiving employer, supplier, carrier, insurer or other counterparty | The recipient's acknowledgment of the specific handoff | Acceptance, completion or payment |
| **Outcome:** "We accepted order O / approved hours H / issued booking B" | Party responsible for that outcome | Its stated decision on that item | Every later step or the truth of all underlying information |

Live Verify authenticates each issuer's statement. A dishonest issuer can still make a false statement. A recipient-issued receipt is stronger evidence of receipt than the intermediary's own delivery assertion because it comes from the party that supposedly received the item.

## Cases, Actors and Demand for Proof

Each row identifies a particular action worth documenting. The last column describes what can usually be omitted from that action receipt, subject to disclosure duties and the recipient's decision needs.

| Case and actors | Middle action and suitable issuer | Who wants proof, and why | Fee boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permanent recruitment:** candidate → recruiter → hiring employer | Employer acknowledges receiving a named candidate for a vacancy through the recruiter, with candidate-authorisation reference and date | Candidate checks that an application reached the real employer; recruiter supports an introduction claim; employer investigates duplicate submissions | Employer–recruiter commission can be separate. Receipt does not decide which recruiter earned a fee |
| **Temporary staffing and outsourced payroll:** worker → staffing agency → hirer; agency → payroll employer/provider → worker | Hirer confirms approved hours for a period; payroll provider separately acknowledges receiving those hours | Worker traces a missing-pay problem; agency shows it passed approved hours onward; hirer checks what it approved; payroll team identifies where processing stopped | Client billing margin can be separate. Worker pay, deductions and charges must remain visible where needed or required |
| **Cross-border labour recruitment:** worker → local recruiter → overseas agency → destination employer | Destination employer confirms job, named recruiting parties and offer reference; each recipient acknowledges the worker's application or documents | Worker and family check that a job exists and documents arrived; employer identifies unauthorised subagents; worker advocate investigates broken promises | No blanket secrecy for worker-paid fees. A fee or reimbursement receipt is a separate, worker-held record |
| **Commercial referrals and sales agents:** buyer → introducer → seller | Seller acknowledges an identified prospect or qualified referral under an agreed programme reference | Agent supports attribution; seller's sales and accounts teams resolve duplicate claims; buyer checks who represents whom | Commission schedule can remain in the parties' agreement. Receipt alone does not prove causation, exclusivity or entitlement |
| **Property sales and lettings:** buyer/tenant → agent → seller/landlord | Seller/landlord or an independently authorised receiving office acknowledges a specific offer or application version | Applicant wants assurance the offer was passed on; owner wants the actual terms; agent demonstrates handling | Agency commission can be separate. Offer amount and conditions belong in the offer receipt when they are the matter being checked |
| **Insurance placement:** insured → broker → insurer/underwriter | Insured confirms broker authority; insurer separately acknowledges submission, then issues any binding/cover confirmation | Insured checks that a renewal was submitted and whether cover was actually bound; broker distinguishes its submission from insurer delay; underwriter checks authority | Broker remuneration can be separate where permitted. Receipt of a proposal is explicitly not insurance cover |
| **Procurement and sourcing:** business buyer → sourcing agent → manufacturer/supplier | Supplier acknowledges the buyer's identified purchase-order version, specification, quantities and agreed milestone | Buyer checks that the agent placed the intended order; agent proves the handoff; finance checks the invoice against supplier acceptance | Agent commission can be separate. Buyer-facing price and material terms cannot be omitted when those are what the buyer needs to verify |
| **Freight forwarding and customs:** shipper/importer → forwarder/broker → carrier/customs authority | Carrier issues booking or custody acknowledgment; customs authority issues submission acceptance or clearance evidence where available | Importer checks that a declaration exists; shipper distinguishes booked space from goods handed over; forwarder shows the point of downstream delay | Forwarding margin can be separate. Relevant duties, declared values and shipment details remain in the appropriate records |
| **Travel agencies and booking platforms:** traveller/company → agent/platform → hotel/airline | Hotel confirms reservation; airline confirms ticket issuance, with dates, service and booking references | Traveller checks that the supplier recognises the booking; corporate travel desk checks service; agent shows supplier acceptance | Supplier net rate or commission can be separate. Customer total and refund entitlement stay in customer-facing terms |
| **Talent, literary and licensing agents:** creator → agent → publisher, producer or licensee | Recipient acknowledges a work submission; contracting party confirms a deal; payment recipient acknowledges a royalty remittance | Creator checks that work reached the intended recipient; agent proves a submission; accountant traces royalties between organisations | Agent percentage can be separate from submission proof. Creator-facing settlement needs the amounts and deductions needed for reconciliation |
| **Escrow and settlement:** buyer/seller → escrow or settlement agent → beneficiary/bank | Escrow holder confirms an authorised release; receiving institution separately confirms receipt when it can | Beneficiary distinguishes release instruction from arrival of funds; payer checks recipient and transaction; agent answers a missing-funds complaint | Service fee can be separate from release proof. Payment amount, currency and recipient are material to a funds receipt |
| **Ordinary employment through internal intermediaries:** employee → manager/HR/shared service → payroll, insurer or training provider | Downstream team/provider acknowledges an approved overtime request, expense claim, benefit enrolment or training booking | Employee checks that "HR is handling it" became a real submission; manager proves approval reached processing; provider identifies the pending stage | No commission is necessary. The same problem arises from delegated work and handoffs inside an organisation |

## Recruitment: What a Jobseeker Wants to Know

"We are on the client's preferred supplier list" and "we sent your details to the client" require **two different client-issued claims**. Membership of a recruitment trade body is neither of these. Preferred-supplier status also does not necessarily mean the agency is instructed on this vacancy.

The actors are the candidate, named recruiter, agency legal entity, hiring client and, sometimes, a managed-service provider or master vendor operating the client's recruitment intake. Where that service issues the claim, identify its delegated role: receipt by the service is not necessarily receipt by the hiring manager.

| Possible misleading claim or conduct | Evidence the candidate would want | Issuer and remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| Invented preferred-supplier relationship | Current appointment naming client, agency entity/domain, region and role categories | Client procurement or delegated programme operator; does not prove this vacancy is assigned |
| Genuine supplier appointment stretched to another subsidiary or country | Explicit employing entity, geography and covered services | Client; appointment to one group company does not cover the whole group |
| Old appointment presented after removal | Dated, expiring appointment with current verification status | Client; old genuine text alone is insufficient |
| Agency is a subagent but implies direct client access | Named contracting supplier, permitted subagency and receiving route | Client or authorised programme operator; each issuer speaks only for its own delegation |
| Real supplier relationship used to advertise an unassigned or invented vacancy | Vacancy-specific instruction or acknowledgment that this agency may submit for this requisition | Client intake team; does not promise an interview |
| "Sent your CV" when it is still in the agency database | Receipt for the candidate, client, requisition and submission version | Client's actual intake system; agency-issued dispatch is only the agency's statement |
| Submitted to a different client or role | Candidate-held authorisation identifying the intended destination; destination receipt | Candidate consent workflow and receiving client; a receipt alone does not establish consent |
| Submitted without permission, or with altered CV, salary expectation or availability | Candidate-approved submission version plus client receipt bound to that version | Candidate approval record and client; a short receipt must not imply that unbound attachments were checked |
| "Exclusive representation" asserted without agreement | Specific, time-limited candidate authorisation, separate from agency appointment | Candidate's authenticated approval record; employer cannot consent for the candidate |
| "Client rejected you" used to conceal no submission | Client-issued outcome for the same application, if the client offers such receipts | Client; it need not disclose private deliberations, and silence is not rejection |
| Interview or offer invented to retain the candidate | Client-issued invitation or offer, including role, terms and issuer authority | Hiring client; supplier status is not authority to offer employment |
| Two agencies claim the same candidate or first introduction | Both genuine receipts, candidate approvals and relevant dates | Client and candidate records; contract and independently retained evidence decide priority, not the hash alone |
| "Application withdrawn" or "vacancy filled" used to steer the candidate elsewhere | Client acknowledgment of withdrawal or current vacancy status where available | Client; do not disclose another candidate's identity |

The candidate should receive the appropriate receipts directly or through an agreed delivery route, rather than needing the recruiter's permission to retrieve them. A client can offer a narrow acknowledgment without exposing its entire preferred supplier list, agency fee schedule or applicant pool. Its agreement with the agency should define what the acknowledgment means for fee attribution.

## Estate Agents, Realtors and Letting Agents

The actors are the owner/seller/landlord, agency, individual negotiator, buyer/prospective tenant, and any lawyer, settlement agent, deposit custodian or protection scheme. Agency models and duties differ by jurisdiction. The following are evidence-design cases, not a statement that every agent owes every duty everywhere.

| Possible misleading claim or conduct | Evidence the affected person would want | Issuer and remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| Claims to represent an owner without appointment | Property-specific marketing or negotiating authority, named agency and term | Owner or genuinely delegated property manager; agency self-assertion is weaker |
| Genuine agency brand used by an unauthorised individual | Current agency-issued assignment identifying the negotiator and property | Agency; professional membership alone does not prove this assignment |
| "I passed your offer to the seller" when it was withheld | Owner/authorised recipient acknowledgment of the exact offer version | Seller or authorised receiving office; the agent's own dispatch receipt is not seller acknowledgment |
| Offer passed on with changed price, conditions or timing | Buyer-approved offer plus matching recipient acknowledgment | Buyer approval record and seller receipt; a reference alone does not bind full terms |
| "Seller rejected" or invented counteroffer | Seller-issued decision on the same offer reference | Seller or delegated decision-maker; no requirement to reveal private reasoning |
| "Another bidder offered more" | If the seller chooses, a narrowly scoped attestation from the party holding the offer record | Authenticates that party's assertion only; cannot exclude a shill, collusion or fabricated underlying bid |
| "You must use our mortgage broker, lawyer or other referral partner" | Owner's actual conditions, distinct from agency recommendation; applicable disclosures separately | Owner for their conditions; authenticity does not make a condition lawful or fair |
| "Deposit paid", "protected" or "transferred to the landlord" conflated | Separate collection receipt, actual recipient acknowledgment, and scheme protection status where applicable | Each responsible recipient; scheme registration need not mean a scheme physically holds cash |
| Fake or substituted deposit account instructions | Current payment instruction from the authorised payee, tied to applicant and property | Payee; check its authority separately from the instruction's authenticity |
| Delayed refund blamed on the next party | Release instruction and downstream receipt or settlement record | Sender and receiving institution; instructed is not settled |
| "Owner approved these repairs/deductions" | Owner instruction or itemised deduction statement, with supporting documents as needed | Responsible owner/manager; verification does not establish that the charge is justified |
| Property shown as reserved, under offer or withdrawn to steer applicants | Owner-authorised status dated for the property or applicant | Owner/manager; current status can change and is not proof of an eventual sale |

An individual owner need not operate a domain. A property-management service, lawyer or settlement platform can issue an acknowledgment identifying whom it represents and what it actually received. If the issuing office is controlled by the same agent whose conduct is in question, that remains the agent's evidence rather than independent seller confirmation.

### Home-rental deposits: existing coverage

- [Rental Listing and Deposit Authority](view.html?doc=rental-listing-deposit-authority) covers listing authority, payment instructions and holding-deposit receipts.
- [Tenancy Deposit Protection Status](view.html?doc=tenancy-deposit-protection-status) models scheme-issued status using UK examples; it is not a worldwide deposit regime.
- [Deposits Held by Third Parties](view.html?doc=deposits-held-by-third-parties) covers the broader deposit-holder pattern, including rental security deposits.

For this rollup, the derivation is the **handoff between agent and actual destination**. "The agent received the money", "the landlord received it", "a scheme registered protection" and "a custodian holds it" are distinct. In an insured protection arrangement, protection can exist while the landlord or agent retains funds; [TDS explains the distinction between its insured and custodial arrangements](https://custodial.tenancydepositscheme.com/tools-and-guides/faqs/tenants/how-does-tds-protect-my-deposit/). An agent-issued receipt does not independently establish a custodial bank balance. An independently issued holder statement is still that holder's attestation, not a guarantee of solvency or recoverability.

## Other Intermediary Derivations

These are possible misleading claims to design against, not allegations about these professions. Each row also identifies a reason for an honest intermediary to participate.

| Intermediary and actors | Questionable claim to test | Useful independent record and who wants it | Honest-adopter value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mortgage/loan broker: borrower → broker → lender | "Application lodged", "approved", or "lender requires this fee" | Lender application receipt, decision or fee instruction; borrower and broker | Distinguish broker submission from lender delay and stop impersonation |
| Migration/visa adviser: applicant → adviser → authority | "Filed", "approved", or "pay another government charge" | Authority-issued filing receipt, decision or charge notice; applicant | Show real progress without disclosing consultancy margin |
| Education/admissions agent: student → agent → institution | "Authorised university agent", "application submitted", "place guaranteed" | Institution appointment, application receipt and actual offer; student and sponsor | Demonstrate appointment and protect against cloned agents |
| Insurance broker: insured → broker → insurer | "Submitted", "bound", "insurer declined", or "claim paid" | Insurer acknowledgment, cover confirmation, decision or remittance; insured and broker | Reassurance, fewer status calls, proof of diligent placement; no assumption of prevalent dishonesty |
| Sourcing/procurement agent: buyer → agent → supplier | "Factory accepted", "production started", or "supplier raised its price" | Supplier order acceptance or change notice; buyer and finance team | Prove milestones and distinguish supplier changes from agent markup |
| Freight/customs broker: trader → broker → carrier/authority | "Booked", "cleared", or "customs requires a release charge" | Carrier booking/custody receipt or authority notice; trader | Locate delay and authenticate a third party's demand |
| Travel agent: traveller → agent → supplier | "Ticketed", "hotel confirmed", or "supplier has not refunded" | Supplier ticket/reservation and refund record; traveller and corporate desk | Prevent forged itineraries and distinguish supplier delay |
| Talent/literary agent: creator → agent → producer/publisher | "Submitted", "rejected", or "royalties not received" | Recipient submission receipt or remittance record; creator and accountant | Demonstrate work and trace payments while keeping negotiation strategy private |
| Claims-management firm: claimant → representative → insurer/compensation body | "Claim filed", "settlement offered", or "nothing paid yet" | Responsible body's receipt, offer and payment record; claimant | Prove service and prevent impersonation of the representative |
| Debt negotiator/collector: debtor or creditor → intermediary → counterparty | "Creditor accepted the plan", "payment forwarded", or "I have authority to collect" | Creditor appointment/acceptance and recipient payment acknowledgment; debtor and creditor | Prevent unauthorised demands and demonstrate remittance |
| Auction/consignment agent: seller → agent → buyer | "Unsold", "buyer has not paid", or "proceeds remitted" | Auction operator sale record and payment-recipient receipt; consignor | Reconcile sale, collection and remittance; does not expose an undisclosed private bidder |
| Escrow/settlement agent: payer → holder → beneficiary | "Release authorised", "sent", or "beneficiary received" | Authoriser's instruction, holder's release and destination acknowledgment; both principals | Reduce missing-money disputes and prevent forged release instructions |
| Permit/registration expediter: applicant → agent → authority | "Filed", "permit issued", or "official surcharge required" | Authority receipt, permit or charge notice; applicant | Demonstrate filing and distinguish official charges from service charges |

Donation fundraisers, affiliate marketers and advertising resellers can share the pattern: a charity acknowledges remittance, a merchant acknowledges a referral, or a publisher acknowledges a placement. Those records do not prove how much cash was collected, that a sale was caused by the referral, or that ad impressions were human. These are weaker fits when the central question is completeness or statistical fraud rather than document authenticity.

## Adoption by Businesses with Little to Lose

The best initial adopter need not be the sector with the worst misconduct. It may be a reputable intermediary that already performs the promised work, can obtain counterparty receipts and wants clients to see that work. An insurance broker could offer a client a useful trail from appointment through submission to insurer response without publishing its commission contract.

| Adoption position | Examples | Why adoption might appeal | Friction to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action already recorded; little sensitive commercial information needed | Insurer submission acknowledgments, supplier order acceptance, carrier booking confirmations | Better client service, fewer progress inquiries, evidence of performed work | Counterparty must issue portable receipts |
| Honest intermediary needs protection against impersonation | Recruitment supplier appointment, institutional education-agent appointment, property negotiator assignment | A genuine firm can demonstrate relationships a clone cannot obtain | Appointment scope and issuer authority must be clear |
| Subject wants visibility across a fragmented chain | Payroll handoffs, migrant recruitment, rental deposits | Worker/customer locates the stage of delay | Subject access, personal data, and reluctance of some participants |
| Genuine records may expose attribution or financial disputes | Sales referrals, competing recruiters, consignment and royalty remittances | Strong evidence for the honest agent or creator | Contract definitions, disclosure duties, correlated margins and collusion |

These are adoption hypotheses to validate with practitioners. Even an honest agency may resist extra work, revealing its client list, or a receipt that could be misconstrued as accepting fee liability. Offer narrow claims with explicit scope, automate issuance from existing systems, and deliver them to the parties who need them. Do not promise that commercial terms can always remain hidden or that verification replaces accountability.

## Where to Start

These priorities are product judgments, rather than measured market-size estimates:

1. **Approved hours passed to payroll.** Repeated, bounded claims; institutional issuers; a worker has a concrete reason to check. The worker should receive both the hirer's approval and the payroll recipient's acknowledgment. Neither is a payslip or proof of payment.
2. **Supplier acceptance of an agent-placed order.** Both buyer and agent benefit, and the supplier already has an order system from which to issue the acknowledgment. Especially useful when the confirmation travels beyond that system.
3. **Supplier confirmation of an agent-made booking.** A traveller carries the proof to a different company or country. Existing supplier lookup is often sufficient; Live Verify adds value when the confirmation must travel as text or paper.
4. **Employer acknowledgment of a recruiter submission.** Direct candidate benefit and potential recruiter benefit. Adoption is harder when an employer fears acknowledging receipt will be interpreted as accepting fee liability; the claim should explicitly reserve that question.

Referrals are attractive to commission earners but harder as a first deployment: contracts may define an introduction narrowly, earlier relationships may exist, and two agents may both have genuine receipts. Verification preserves the evidence for the dispute; it does not settle it.

## Data Verified

Issuer, principal or subject reference, intermediary identity, receiving party, specific action, transaction or assignment reference, document version, scope, event date and time, qualifications, and a random receipt ID.

Use precise verbs: **received**, **approved**, **accepted**, **issued**, **released**, or **paid**, with the object of the action. Avoid an unexplained "completed" spanning several organisations. Include quantities, amounts or deadlines whenever they are necessary to establish the particular action. A reference to a separate document is not itself verification of that document's contents.

## Fees and Commercial Privacy

Issue separate, purpose-specific claims for appointment, action receipt and financial settlement. They can share a transaction reference without sharing all commercial terms. Each is an issuer-authored document with its own hash; this does not require redacting an already hashed document or adding selective-disclosure cryptography.

The intermediary can carry a supplier-issued receipt without showing its commission agreement. The customer can carry an authority claim without showing the intermediary's wholesale price. However, issuing both gross-price and net-price claims to the same audience can reveal the margin by subtraction. References and repeated receipts can also expose commercial relationships.

Confidential commercial margin is different from hiding charges owed by a customer or deductions from a worker. A fee-free action receipt must not be presented as a complete financial account. Where a worker, customer, auditor or regulator is entitled to the amounts, they need the appropriate financial record.

## Second-Party Use

The second party depends on the document: an appointed agent receives the principal's authority claim; a submitting agent receives the counterparty's receipt; a worker receives an assignment or payroll acknowledgment. State the recipient for each claim rather than treating the intermediary as issuer by default.

**Intermediaries** carry acknowledgments to show the work they performed, defend against downstream-delay allegations, and support agreed milestone billing. **Workers, candidates and customers** keep the receipts concerning them so checking progress does not depend entirely on the intermediary's cooperation.

## Third-Party Use

**Accounts and procurement teams** compare accepted orders, approved hours and settlement records before acting. **Other agents in a chain** check the specific upstream authority or downstream handoff they rely on. **Auditors, worker representatives and dispute handlers** examine the available records with the holder's permission. They still need the relevant contract and underlying evidence to decide liability or commission entitlement.

## Verification Architecture

The principal issues authority; the intermediary may issue its action statement; the recipient issues its receipt or decision. Each uses its own domain and retains responsibility for its statement. An intermediary-hosted lookup is not independent counterparty confirmation merely because it names the counterparty.

Deliver receipts to the affected person as well as the intermediary where agreed and appropriate. Otherwise an intermediary can withhold the very evidence needed to challenge it. A user without an institutional domain can hold and verify a claim without operating an endpoint. Individual sellers or landlords without endpoints may need an established receiving service; the claim must identify that service's actual authority and what it observed.

This is a sequence of transaction records. It is distinct from an [authority chain](../../docs/authority-chain-spec.md), which establishes who endorses an issuer. A referral between companies need not reach a sovereign root to be useful; the relevant employer, supplier or counterparty can be the trusted commercial source.

## Verification Response and Time

Use the existing [verification response format](../../docs/Verification-Response-Format.md) for terse issuer status. Business details belong in the hashed text, not a public response echo. A valid lookup authenticates the statement's wording; a revoked or restricted result calls for investigation. A missing entry does not prove that an action never happened.

For authority, check expiry and current revocation. For historical receipts, preserve the acknowledgment as an event record and handle corrections explicitly rather than silently rewriting it. Today's lookup alone does not prove what an endpoint returned on an earlier date. Disputes over priority or backdating may require independently retained verification receipts or witnessing; a date printed inside a hash is still an issuer's assertion. See [point-in-time vs current](../../docs/point-in-time-vs-current.md) and [witnessing third parties](../../docs/WITNESSING-THIRD-PARTIES.md).

## Privacy Salt

Include an issuer-generated unpredictable receipt ID, preferably at least 128 random bits, in the hashed text. Names, vacancy numbers and hours alone are guessable. Share personal receipts with the people who need them; do not publish a searchable worker or candidate register. A salted hash reduces enumeration risk but does not prevent a recipient from forwarding the document or an endpoint operator from observing lookups.

## Competition and Limits

| Existing approach | Where it is sufficient | Possible Live Verify contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient portal, ATS, procurement or payroll system | Both parties have access and can inspect the authoritative record | Portable acknowledgment for someone outside that system |
| EDI/API integration | Organisations already exchange authenticated structured records | Human-readable proof for workers, customers or external dispute handlers |
| Signed email or digitally signed PDF | Signature remains intact and recipients can validate it | Verification after the claim travels as copied text or paper |
| Forwarded email or screenshot | Low-stakes coordination between trusted people | Check attribution and alterations against the named issuer |
| Contract and accounting records | Determining fees, pay, liability and settlement | Authenticating particular records used in that determination |

No receipt proves that all offers were forwarded, all candidates were considered, all royalty sales were reported, or all payments were made. Completeness needs a separately defined and auditable record set. Colluding parties can issue matching false statements. A verified referral also cannot enforce a non-circumvention agreement or automatically collect commission.

- **International recruitment:** the [ILO fair recruitment principles and fee guidance](https://www.ilo.org/publications/general-principles-and-operational-guidelines-fair-recruitment-and) address abusive recruitment and recruitment fees. Fee privacy must be assessed against worker protection, not only agency preference.
- **International travel:** [IATA's agent identification guidance](https://www.iata.org/en/services/travel-agency-program/tids/faq/) distinguishes agent identification from ticket-issuing capability. Knowing who an agent is does not confirm a particular booking.
- **International trade:** [ICC's documentary collection guidance](https://2go.iccwbo.org/explore-our-products/a-practical-guide-to-documentary-collections-understanding-documentary-collections-in-international-trade.html) describes separate roles for remitting and presenting banks, illustrating why "sent", "received" and "released" are different events.

## Existing Coverage and Further Derivations

Existing documents cover many adjacent objects. The addition here is proof of a **particular middle action**, preferably acknowledged by its recipient:

- [Recruitment agency verification](view.html?doc=recruitment-agency-verification) establishes membership; [employment authority](view.html?doc=employment-authority-confirmations) establishes who may make offers. Neither alone proves a candidate submission reached an employer.
- [Pay stubs and payslips](view.html?doc=pay-stubs-payslips) cover payroll output. Approved-hours and payroll-input receipts explain earlier handoffs.
- [Insurance broker authority](view.html?doc=insurance-broker-letter-of-authority) covers appointment; submission receipts and insurer-issued cover confirmations concern subsequent actions.
- [Purchase orders and delivery notes](view.html?doc=purchase-orders-delivery-notes), [customs entry and clearance](view.html?doc=customs-entry-clearance), and [CMR consignment notes](view.html?doc=cmr-consignment-notes) provide transaction and custody anchors.
- [Travel agency invoices](view.html?doc=travel-agency-invoices) and [hotel booking confirmations](view.html?doc=hotel-booking-confirmation-verification) let the agency's and supplier's statements be distinguished.
- [Publishing contracts and royalties](view.html?doc=publishing-contracts-royalties) and [escrow releases](view.html?doc=escrow-releases) cover financial outcomes; intervening remittance receipts can help trace the gaps.

Recruiter appointments, candidate submission receipts, property offer receipts and approved-hours handoff receipts are the four illustrated derivations. The recruitment, property and other intermediary sections above keep further permutations together in this rollup; they can be developed into standalone use cases when adoption evidence warrants it.

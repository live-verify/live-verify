WITNESSING

Issuer-fed witnessing

In regulated sectors a witness can be fed by the issuer itself. A regulator requires the issuer to send every hash it publishes, and every later change, to an independent firm. This is a business in its own right and a benefit to the issuer, the regulator and the public.

Status: a design, not a service. Witnessing sits outside the core Live Verify specification. No witnessing organisation operates today, the reference apps have no witness plugin, and any company names or prices on these pages are illustrations.

How it differs from the plugin model

Chosen by the person checkingFed by the issuer
Who engages the witnessThe person checking, through a plugin in their appThe issuer, because a regulator or contract requires it
Who paysThe person checking, or their firm, solicitor or insurerThe issuer, as a cost of compliance
Does the issuer know?No. The witness makes an ordinary public lookupYes. The issuer sends the hashes
What the witness receivesOne hash, its address, the issuer's answer and the timeEvery hash and every status change, as they happen
CoverageOnly the documents that person checkedEverything the issuer publishes, continuously
Main valueProtection in an individual disputeA regulatory audit trail and non-repudiation at scale

What the issuer-fed witness holds

  • Every hash the issuer publishes, as it is published.
  • Every later change: a new status, a revocation, or a deletion. A deletion is itself recorded, which the plugin model cannot do.
  • Never the document's text, in whole or in part. No names, amounts, dates or other fields are sent. As in the plugin model, the witness cannot read what it has witnessed.

Who benefits

  • The regulator can inspect one independent record of everything a firm has attested to, without relying on the firm's own account of its history.
  • The issuer gains proof of what it did and did not issue, which protects it against forged documents bearing its name and against claims that it altered its records.
  • Holders and checkers gain a record the issuer cannot quietly rewrite, and a second place to confirm a hash if the issuer's own service is unavailable.
  • The witness has a continuing contract with each issuer it serves, which is a steadier business than occasional attestations.
